# myWorld

**Personal Digital Environment**  
*Create. Organize. Preserve.*

---

myWorld is a personal digital environment concept designed to collect, organize, preserve and develop different forms of personal content.

It combines the ideas of a digital diary, a personal archive and a creative workspace, allowing users to build an environment that reflects their individual needs and interests.

Rather than imposing a predefined organizational structure, myWorld explores a flexible system in which users can create personalized sections, manage collections, connect content and organize independent projects within a unified environment.

> **Project focus:** Software Engineering · Software Architecture · Modular Systems · Digital Content Management

## Overview

myWorld starts from a simple idea: creating a digital environment where individuals can collect their memories, preserve their creations, organize their ideas and develop personal projects.

Users can build their own digital space, creating dedicated sections for different interests and activities.

Examples of possible sections include:

- Personal diaries and memories.
- Stories, poems and written works.
- Music, beats and audio recordings.
- Photographs and visual collections.
- Ideas and personal notes.
- Creative and technical projects.
- Documents and references.

The concept combines three complementary dimensions:

| Dimension | Purpose |
|:--|:--|
| **Digital Diary** | Preserve personal memories, experiences and reflections. |
| **Personal Archive** | Collect and organize different types of digital content. |
| **Creative Workspace** | Gather materials and develop ideas or projects. |

As a software engineering case study, myWorld explores:

- Modular and extensible architecture.
- Flexible data modeling.
- Digital content management.
- Customizable user environments.
- Relationships between content and workspaces.
- Cross-platform software design.

The project focuses on the analysis and design of the system rather than its full implementation.

## Vision & Philosophy

> *Your space, your structure.*

myWorld aims to provide an environment that adapts to its users rather than forcing them to follow predefined organizational rules.

Its design is guided by three principles:

| Principle | Purpose |
|:--|:--|
| **Freedom** | Allow users to shape their own digital environment. |
| **Creativity** | Support the organization and development of personal content. |
| **Organization** | Make different types of information accessible and manageable. |

The objective is to bring personal content, creative activities and independent projects together without sacrificing organizational freedom.

Users can shape their environment according to their preferences, interests and workflows.

## Conceptual Structure

myWorld revolves around a personal homepage and a collection of customizable sections and independent projects.

### 01 — Home

The main entry point to the user's digital environment.

The homepage provides quick access to recently opened elements, allowing users to resume previous activities without navigating through multiple sections.

Its conceptual elements include:

- Recently opened projects.
- Recently accessed sections.
- Quick access to personal content.
- Pinned projects and shortcuts.

The homepage prioritizes recent activity rather than enforcing a rigid organizational structure.

Users can independently decide how to organize and arrange their content within the other areas of the environment.

### 02 — My Sections

A dedicated area containing all personal sections created by the user.

Each section represents an independent environment that can be customized according to its purpose.

Users can create new sections, select predefined templates and organize their content according to their preferences.

### 03 — Projects

A dedicated area for managing independent personal projects.

Projects function as autonomous workspaces, bringing together related contents from different sections and collections.

For example, opening a project called *Darkness* could provide access to:

- Musical beats.
- Written lyrics.
- Cover artwork.
- Personal notes.
- References and inspiration.
- Related materials.

A project does not depend on the physical location of its contents within the environment.

This concept distinguishes where content is stored from how it is used within a particular activity.

## Personal Sections

> *Build your own digital environment.*

Sections are customizable spaces that users can create to organize different aspects of their personal lives and interests.

Each section starts from a predefined template that establishes its initial structure and purpose. Users can then customize its appearance and organization.

The template concepts include:

| Template | Purpose |
|:--|:--|
| **Diary** | An environment focused on texts, dates and photographs. |
| **Archive** | A space for collecting, categorizing and preserving content. |
| **Creative** | An environment focused on creative materials and projects. |

Possible customization dimensions include:

- Layout and visual organization.
- Colors and backgrounds.
- Fonts and typography.
- Icons and visual elements.
- Section structure.

Templates provide a starting point without restricting individual customization.

## Content Management

> *Organize without unnecessary restrictions.*

myWorld explores a unified model for managing different types of digital content.

| Content Type | Description |
|:--|:--|
| **Text** | Notes, stories, poems, lyrics and personal writings. |
| **Images** | Photographs, illustrations, artwork and visual references. |
| **Audio** | Music, beats, recordings and other audio files. |
| **Video** | Personal videos and audiovisual materials. |
| **Documents** | Files, documents and other digital resources. |
| **Links** | External references and useful resources. |

### Collections

Users can organize content through collections, folders and subfolders.

Collections provide a hierarchical organizational structure while remaining conceptually independent of sections.

Content does not have to belong to a collection. Individual elements may remain directly within a section whenever appropriate.

### Tags & Relationships

Tags provide an additional way to classify and retrieve content.

A single element may have multiple tags and be associated with multiple sections.

Related contents can also be connected independently of their original locations.

For example, a musical beat could be associated with:

- Music.
- Creative Projects.
- Personal Ideas.

These associations should reference the same content rather than create unnecessary duplicates.

This approach allows different organizational structures to coexist within the same environment.

## Projects & Workspaces

> *Everything related, in one place.*

Projects are independent workspaces designed to bring together materials associated with a specific activity or idea.

Unlike ordinary folders, projects are not limited to a hierarchical storage structure.

A project can conceptually aggregate different types of content, including texts, images, audio files, documents and references, regardless of the sections in which they are originally organized.

For example:

**Darkness — Music Project**

- Beats and audio productions.
- Lyrics and written material.
- Cover artwork.
- Creative references.
- Personal notes.

Projects can be considered independent views over related content.

This model emphasizes the distinction between where content is stored and how it is used within a particular activity.

## Customization & Extensibility

> *Adapt the environment to individual needs.*

myWorld explores multiple levels of customization.

### Visual Customization

Users can modify the appearance of their sections through visual configuration, without requiring programming knowledge.

### Structural Customization

Users can create sections, choose templates, organize content and modify the structure of their personal environment.

### Custom Development

For technically skilled users, the concept includes the possibility of creating more extensively personalized sections through web technologies.

Custom development is optional and is not required for ordinary use.

### Modular Tools

Advanced tools are considered through a modular approach.

| Level | Description |
|:--|:--|
| **Core Capabilities** | Essential features that define the environment. |
| **Optional Modules** | Additional tools that users may choose to activate. |
| **External Services** | Dedicated services that can support specialized operations. |

This distinction provides a basis for evaluating modularity, integration boundaries and system complexity.

## Multiplatform Considerations

myWorld is conceived as a digital environment that can be accessed through desktop and mobile devices.

The conceptual design considers how the experience should adapt to the capabilities and characteristics of each platform.

### Desktop

A more extensive working environment, particularly suited to:

- Advanced content editing.
- Project organization.
- Extensive customization.
- Specialized tools.

### Mobile

An experience focused on accessibility and everyday use, particularly suited to:

- Browsing personal content.
- Writing and editing notes.
- Recording or accessing audio.
- Managing collections.
- Accessing projects and sections.

The main design question is how to maintain continuity between devices while adapting the interaction model to each environment.

## Engineering Focus

myWorld provides a broad domain for exploring architectural complexity and system boundaries.

### Domain Modeling

Identify the relationships between users, sections, content items, collections, tags and projects.

### Data Architecture

Evaluate how content references, collections and cross-sectional relationships can be represented without unnecessary duplication.

### Modularity

Define boundaries between core capabilities and optional tools.

### Customization

Analyze how user-defined structures and layouts can be represented while preserving system consistency.

### Cross-Platform Design

Consider platform-independent domain logic and platform-specific interaction requirements.

### Quality Attributes

Evaluate maintainability, usability, extensibility, data integrity and other relevant quality attributes.
